FAQs.
What is my design philosophy?

I design with curiosity and honesty, and a strong belief that the user knows more about their own problem than I do. Before forming opinions about how something should work, I try to understand how it actually works for the people using it. No preconceived notions, no prescribed solutions. (I apply this same principle to movies: I refuse to watch trailers. Cold turkey, every time.)

What drives me is making a real difference for real people — government contractors wrestling with procurement tools, property managers drowning in data, business owners trying to get clarity on their cash flow. The user's context is the design constraint that matters most.

What is my design process?

I start on two tracks at once: working backwards from a deadline to build a project timeline, while pursuing real user anecdotes and background context from internal sources. When I research, I ask unbiased, consistent questions — ones that surface how people actually behave, not ones that lead them toward a solution I've already imagined.

I prototype from day one. That means napkin sketches to quickly externalize a direction, and Figma prototypes calibrated to the right fidelity for the audience — rough for early alignment, polished for engineering handoff.

My background in software development keeps me honest: I aim for the intersection of aspirational and buildable. Launching something real sooner beats shipping something perfect late — and I know enough about implementation to know where that line actually is.
