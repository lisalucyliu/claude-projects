context.

Private Marketplace (PMP) lets administrators inside organizations build customized catalogs of pre-approved products from AWS Marketplace. Admins curate what their users can buy, customize the experience with their company's branding, and control access at the organizational unit or account level. End users subscribe to products from within this curated environment, and can request additions that admins must approve.

The JWCC/C2E defense contract specifically requires that Intelligence Community (ICMP) users purchase AWS Marketplace products through the PMP experience — since governmental entities only want their users buying pre-approved products. But there was a critical problem: PMP existed only on the website version of AWS Marketplace, and ICMP users working inside SCIFs cannot access the public internet. PMP had to move into the AWS console.

Since we had to migrate and rebuild the entire experience anyway, I took this as an opportunity to completely redesign it — the existing experience was stylistically outdated, non-compliant with Cloudscape design standards, and full of poorly built pages that had been implemented without any design oversight.

The contract requirement

ICMP users must buy through PMP. PMP only runs on the public web. ICMP users can't use the public web inside SCIFs.

The state of the product

7 years old. Not Cloudscape-compliant. Many pages were built by engineering without any design input. Scope was misrepresented as 10-15 pages; the actual scope was 2-3x that.

The opportunity

Since we were rebuilding it anyway, I redesigned every surface — introducing new page concepts, new navigation, new patterns, and new standards that cascaded across Marketplace.

Engineering that didn't understand Cloudscape

The engineering team built their own proposed wireframes before design involvement — using Cloudscape components incorrectly throughout. I had to understand why they made those decisions before I could redesign properly, which meant a significant amount of rework just to understand the starting point. I pushed back consistently and guided them toward correct component usage throughout the project.

Scope that was 2-3x what I was told

The original scope was framed as a simple 1-to-1 redesign of 10-15 pages. The actual scope was 2-3x that number, including flows that had never had any design input — product and engineering had simply built them on their own. I discovered these gaps progressively and redesigned them from scratch as I found them.

part 1 — homepage, getting started.

[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER ]
New "My Private Marketplace" landing page — new page I introduced

A new landing page: "My Private Marketplace"

Previously, a mix of essential information and announcements was crammed onto the dashboard page, causing information overload for new users. I introduced a new page — "My Private Marketplace" — as a dedicated starting point for all users. It serves as the place where admins can post special announcements and users arrive with context before they start browsing. I convinced the team that users needed this starting point, and it became a standard part of the new experience.

End user getting started experience — redesigned from scratch

Redesigned end user experience

I rewrote all the verbiage on the main marketing landing homepage, completely redesigned the end user "getting started" page, and designed a new widget to preview what a product looks like when approved for purchase in the PMP environment.

[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER ]
Old branding toolbar vs. new approved-pill approach on products

Removing the non-compliant branding toolbar

The existing experience had a badly implemented extra toolbar that admins could customize with their company logo and colors. It was not Cloudscape compliant, and there was no way to check WCAG color contrast compliance for the header text against whatever color the admin chose. After many discussions with the team, I determined this entire toolbar was unnecessary and replaced it with a small branded pill on each purchasable product — simple, compliant, and effective.

part 2 — admin experience.

The admin experience is where most of the complexity lived. I redesigned the left navigation, the main dashboard, multiple wizard flows, and an entire new suite of pages — and wrote all the help panel content and page descriptions for every page, since 95% of existing pages had none.

Left navigation redesign

I redesigned the left navigation for the entire admin experience, merging some pages and moving others to sit within existing pages. The original navigation had grown organically and no longer reflected how admins actually moved through their tasks.

A new suite of pages for change tracking

I designed an entirely new set of pages to track a hierarchical system of changes implemented by admins — something that had never existed in PMP before. Admins needed visibility into what had changed, when, and by whom. I designed this from scratch, including the IA and the individual page designs.

Revamped wizard flows

I revamped multiple wizard flows to follow Cloudscape guidance precisely, use cleaner language, and work as intended. I also merged certain wizard flows that served similar enough purposes to warrant combination. These flows had originally been implemented without design oversight, and it showed.

part 3 — settings page and new standard.

[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER ]
New settings page with submenu — scrollable, extensible

A new settings standard, adopted across Marketplace

I redesigned the PMP settings page and introduced a submenu structure within the page — enabling future scrollability as more settings sections get added over time. This approach to settings architecture was new to Marketplace. It worked so well that I applied the same pattern in my AWS Data Exchange Data Grants work, where it also became the standard for the ADX settings page.

Complete redesign overview

This is the full scope of what shipped: a new landing page I introduced, a redesigned end user getting started flow, a branding pill replacing the non-compliant toolbar, a reorganized admin navigation, a redesigned dashboard with proper Cloudscape tables, a new change tracking suite of pages, revamped wizard flows, and a new settings architecture. Every page includes help panel content and description verbiage I wrote from scratch.

results.

After almost a year of work and 5+ design approval sessions, the entire new Private Marketplace console experience is live. This may have been the biggest design undertaking I have ever completed. The settings submenu pattern I introduced has since been adopted across other AWS Marketplace products.

For ICMP users working in SCIFs, Private Marketplace now works. That was the whole point.

Beyond launch

Future improvements I'd advocate for: a formal admin onboarding flow for new PMP administrators; improved branding customization options that are Cloudscape-compliant and WCAG-verifiable; and deeper analytics for admins to understand which products their users are actually using vs. requesting.

learnings.
  • Scope misrepresentation is a design problem, not just a project problem. When I discovered the actual scope was 2-3x what I was told, I didn't stop — I mapped everything that existed, identified what needed design, and worked through it. Having a clear view of the total scope, even an uncomfortable one, is the only way to actually plan.
  • Engineering needs design guidance, not just design deliverables. Pushing back on incorrect Cloudscape usage wasn't optional — if I hadn't, the final product would have been non-compliant and difficult to maintain. Being present and specific in that feedback was part of the job.
  • Introducing a new page takes more than good design rationale. Getting "My Private Marketplace" accepted required multiple discussions and a clear articulation of the user problem it solved. The best solution doesn't ship just because it's the best solution.
  • Patterns that work in one product can solve problems in another. The settings submenu I introduced in PMP solved a real information architecture problem. When I encountered a similar problem in Data Grants, I already had the solution. Building reusable patterns is worth the upfront investment.
