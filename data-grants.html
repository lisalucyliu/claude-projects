Data Exchange Grants

Share and manage AWS data entitlements with trusted organizations without going through AWS Marketplace monetization.

Client

AWS Data Exchange (in-house)

My Role

Senior UX Designer

Duration

V1: 5 months (Apr–Nov 2023)

Launched

V1: AWS Re:Invent 2023

context.

AWS Data Exchange (ADX) is a service that helps AWS customers share and manage data entitlements from other organizations at scale. ADX has the hierarchy, delivery, hosting, and security infrastructure within the AWS suite — a great place to store and manage data.

The problem: ADX was originally built to create and deliver monetized data products through AWS Marketplace. That meant customers had to register for Marketplace, set up financial flows, and publicly list at least one product — just to transfer data, even when no money was changing hands.

According to prospective and existing ADX customers, there are 2 major reasons they want to use ADX for delivery without monetizing via AWS Marketplace:

Use case 1: bill outside AWS

Customers want to deliver data via ADX but bill outside AWS. Many already have their own billing system set up and don't want to overhaul it just to use ADX.

Use case 2: internal data sharing

Customers don't want to charge money at all — they're exchanging datasets internally and want to use ADX's hierarchy and infrastructure to organize and transfer data without needing Marketplace.

ADX data grants: a lightweight method of sharing data that runs parallel to the existing ADX/Marketplace data product path — no monetization required.

challenges.

Data Grants was built in 2 projects. V1 (Apr–Nov 2023) was the main effort — multiple new pages plus redesigns of the Entitled Data page and main navigation, launching at Re:Invent 2023. V2 (Apr–Nov 2024) API-fied the experience and improved backend processes, launching at Re:Invent 2024.

Development limitations

I was designing around ADX's existing architecture, with optimization as a secondary goal. I optimized when I could — including successfully convincing the team to remove an unnecessary product details page for data grants, since there's no monetary context. This cost zero additional engineering time.

Navigating AWS's design processes

AWS's size demands a system of checkpoints requiring sign-off from product, design, and engineering leadership at multiple points — each taking 1–2 weeks. For design, UX sign-off and Fit & Finish require special "bar raiser" reviewers. For V1, I launched in November 2023, obtained design bar-raiser approval by September, and applied in July — 2 months before each milestone.

How do we differentiate between new and existing features?

ADX was always synonymous with monetized data products. Adding a parallel, non-monetized path required rethinking the IA at the service level — not just adding a new page.

V1 — requirements & research.

The goal of V1 (6–7 months) was to create the main data grants feature: all new pages, plus modifications to key existing pages and the ADX left navigation. I worked closely with product and development throughout.

Initial name: TOS (tech-only shares)

Data Grants started as the "tech-only program" (TOP) — a lightweight specialized path to fast-track data access without the formalities of ADX and Marketplace. We initially planned for mostly programmatic (API-only) access.

Scale of this feature?

The PM and I weren't sure how big this feature would be. Initially pitched as "lightweight" — are data grants simple enough to live within the Entitled Data page, or do they need their own layer of metadata? On the other hand, this is a reforging of ADX's original product offering. Should data grants exist as their own service? Registering a new AWS service takes a very long time, so that was off the table.

Confirming personas and competitors

Generally "data owner" mapped to sender and "data consumer" to receiver, but the existing ADX personas of sellers and buyers weren't an exact match. I also investigated the overlap with AWS Datazone — key differences being that Datazone organizes data by business domains inside a single large organization and is marketed primarily toward internal usage.

Soft requirements

Many requirements weren't confirmed. I designed multiple options for many pages so I could seek clarification by demoing them to users and engineers. Examples I later clarified: 1 share to 1 dataset or many (answer: 1 to 1 only); whether grants need a human-readable name (answer: no, account ID is sufficient); whether ADX needs to link to a contract (answer: no, both parties contract outside AWS).

User interviews with existing customers

The PM and I co-led 14–15 video calls with existing ADX seller customers. Key findings: most customers need only 1 dataset per 1 share; many have specific billing configurations they can't change; each shared dataset needs metadata about the sending process for audit purposes; the sender always initiates — the receiver never requests.

V1 — design.

I designed concurrently alongside research and requirement-gathering, clarifying requirements through quick mockups and user interviews as I went.

Entitled Data page: an opportunity to redesign

The original Entitled Data page had 4 nested layers (1 click each), was hardcoded at 280px wide (truncating names), and was not Cloudscape-compliant. Since data grants don't have a subscription layer, I pushed to redesign it as a flat table sorted by datasets — clearing the Cloudscape issue and removing extra clicks at no additional engineering cost.

Left navigation: an opportunity to redesign

Adding data grants to the left navigation gave me a chance to rethink the full ordering. Key decisions: separate Data Grants and Data Products into clearly labeled sections; spotlight data access at the top as the most important pages for both personas; reorganize Data Products into persona-specific subsections no longer emphasizing Marketplace purchase phases.

What to name the unit?

We needed a strong verbal identity to differentiate this new feature. "Tech-only shares" was too generic. I brainstormed alternatives and held a workshop with product and engineering to vote. Interestingly, the final name — "data grants" — got zero votes during the workshop but emerged afterward as the best fit.

V1 wireframes

The scope of V1 included creating and viewing data grants (sent and received), redesigning Entitled Data, and redesigning the left navigation.

Create new data grant

When a sender lands on the Sent Data Grants page, they see a list of first-time steps and a table of previously created grants. The creation wizard asks them to select the dataset, give it a unique name and description, enter the receiver's AWS account ID, and specify when access ends. Users can track the "in progress" status on both the details page and the main table.

Receive new data grant

When a receiver lands on the Received Data Grants page, they see a table containing all grants shared with them that require action. Once accepted or expired, grants move to a second tab. Upon acceptance, the link to the entitled dataset becomes active — the receiver now has the right to access and use the data.

Entitled data

The receiver accesses their allowed datasets in the Entitled Data page. For V1, this page was redesigned into a table experience sorted by datasets first, with the AWS Marketplace product or data grant shown in a separate "source" column. For the current version, each data grant only has 1 dataset, so there's no separate source layer — the receiver can access grant details directly from the dataset page.

New navigation

I spotlighted data access and Data Grants in the topmost section, as data grants represent a purer form of data sharing important to both personas. The bottom section contains all Data Product-specific pages, reordered into persona-specific subsections that no longer emphasize the 2 stages of buyer shopping. I also renamed certain pages and subsections to clarify their relevance to AWS Marketplace data products only.

V2 — design.

The goal of V2 was to API-fy the data grants feature, fix things we couldn't implement in V1, and add the ability to redistribute a received data grant. In August 2023, the ADX team was dispersed to other service teams. I rejoined temporarily in 2024 to support the V2 launch.

A 3rd persona: the redistributed (secondary) receiver

V2 added a secondary receiver who gains access through AWS License Manager via redistribution from a primary receiver. This secondary receiver gets access through Entitled Data only — they can't view the received grant on the main received grants page, can't see detailed grant information, and can't redistribute further. This also required a new settings page for senders to opt into the License Manager integration.

Updates to existing pages for data sender and receiver

A sender can now allow or disallow redistribution from their primary receiver — I added this to the data grant creation process. A primary receiver can now redistribute their received data grant by going to AWS License Manager from their grant's details page or Entitled Data details page.

Instant data grants

In V1, it took some time for a data grant to finish creating. In V2, data grant creation is now instantaneous — so a grant no longer needs "in progress" as a status and the Activity History tab for viewing incomplete jobs is no longer needed.

Redistribute view

For senders, step 2 of the creation wizard now requires an additional selection to allow or disallow redistribution. For receivers who can redistribute, they can navigate to AWS License Manager straight from their received data grant, as long as they've set it up first. Any secondary receivers won't be able to see the original grant details in their Entitled Data view.

Settings

ADX now requires a settings page to manage the integration with AWS License Manager. A primary receiver gets notified from their received grant's details page to set this up before they can perform the redistribution action.

result.

Developing data grants fundamentally changed AWS Data Exchange from a supporting layer of AWS Marketplace into its own self-sustaining service. ADX now defines data grants as its primary function, with Marketplace monetization as an optional path.

Beyond V2

Although ADX's team is no longer as robust as before, possible additions for V3 include: grouping of grants in the main table; contracting attached to grants; 1+ datasets per grant with additional grouping layers; a way for data consumers to discover and request datasets; and a possible Snowflake integration so data can be shared directly from Snowflake through ADX.

learnings.
  • Large organizations make big moves with far-reaching consequences. What started as "add one alternate path to the product" became "the alternate path is now the main product." I couldn't have predicted that from the beginning.
  • Be audacious and take any opportunity to improve the existing experience. The left nav and Entitled Data pages wouldn't have changed so profoundly if I hadn't insisted it was worth doing.
  • Leadership shakeups produce completely unforeseen results. The team being dispersed after V1 is part of why V2 almost didn't happen — and why I rejoined to see it through.
  • An incredible team makes a technically dense project worth it. I had exceptional product and engineering partners, and I jumped at the chance to work with them again on V2.
